"Local AI" has a reputation for being a hobby for developers — something involving a command line, cryptic model names, and an afternoon of setup. It doesn't have to be. Here's a plain-English guide to running a private AI assistant on your own computer, even if you've never touched a terminal in your life.

## What "running AI locally" actually means

Normally, when you use ChatGPT, the AI isn't on your computer at all. You type a message, it gets sent to a company's servers, the AI thinks there, and the answer comes back. Your words made a round trip across the internet.

**Running AI locally flips that around.** The AI model lives and runs on your own machine. When you ask it something, nothing gets sent anywhere — the thinking happens right there on your laptop or desktop. That's what makes it private: there's no server involved, so there's nothing to leak, store, or train on.

The trade-off is that a model running on your computer isn't quite as powerful as the giant models in the cloud. But for everyday writing, summarizing, brainstorming, and reading documents, modern local models are genuinely excellent — and for anything sensitive, the privacy is worth far more than the last few percent of raw capability.

## What you need

Good news: probably just the computer you already own.

- **A reasonably modern computer.** Most laptops and desktops from the last few years can run a capable local model. More memory helps, but you don't need a gaming rig.
- **A one-time download.** The AI model is a file that gets downloaded once. After that, you can work completely offline.
- **That's it.** No account, no subscription, no API keys.

## The two ways to do it

Broadly, there are two paths.

### Path 1: The developer route (free, technical)

Tools like **Ollama** and **LM Studio** are free and powerful. If you're comfortable with a command line, or you enjoy browsing model catalogs and comparing settings, they give you a lot of control. The catch is that they're built for enthusiasts: you'll be choosing models yourself, managing them, and often wiring up a separate chat interface. For a developer, that's a fun afternoon. For everyone else, it's usually where the project stalls.
