Most people compare AI tools on features. But if you're going to use AI regularly, the more interesting question is what it costs you *over time* — and here the two approaches are completely different. A cloud subscription is a bill that never stops. Running AI [locally](/blog/local-ai-vs-cloud-ai) is something you pay for once. Let's do the honest math on both.

Here's the short version: **a subscription looks cheaper on day one and more expensive every day after.** A one-time purchase is the reverse. Which one wins depends entirely on how long you plan to keep using AI.

## What a cloud subscription really costs

The headline number is easy: ChatGPT Plus is **$20 a month**. That's the tier most people land on once the free version starts hitting limits. Sounds modest — until you annualize it:

- **1 year:** $240
- **2 years:** $480
- **3 years:** $720
- **5 years:** $1,200

And that's just one tool. If you also pay for a Claude subscription, an image generator, or a team plan at work, the monthly line items stack up. The subscription model is designed so the cost is small enough to ignore each month and large enough to matter each year.

There's also a subtler cost that never appears on the invoice: **the meter is always running.** Because you're paying monthly whether you use it or not, there's a quiet pressure to "get your money's worth" — which usually means feeding the tool more of your work, including things you might otherwise have kept private.

## What local AI really costs

Running AI on your own machine flips the model. Instead of renting access to a model on someone else's servers, you download an [open-source model](/blog/which-local-ai-model-should-you-run) and run it on hardware you already own. The costs break down into three honest categories:

- **The software.** A tool like WorkInPrivate is a **one-time $49.99** — no subscription, no per-message fees. You buy it once and it's yours.
- **The models.** The AI models themselves are free. They're open-source and downloaded from Hugging Face at no cost.
- **Electricity.** Running a model uses your computer's processor, which uses power. For typical personal use this is measured in pennies, not dollars — nowhere near a monthly subscription.

The one thing local AI asks of you is a **computer capable of running it** — but if you bought a laptop in the last few years, you very likely already have what you need. That's not a new expense; it's hardware you already paid for.

## The break-even point

Put the two side by side and the crossover is easy to see. A $49.99 one-time purchase pays for itself against a $20/month subscription in **about ten weeks.** Everything after that is money you keep.

| Time using AI | Cloud subscription | Local AI (one-time) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 months | $60 | $49.99 |
| 1 year | $240 | $49.99 |
| 3 years | $720 | $49.99 |
| 5 years | $1,200 | $49.99 |

If you're going to use AI for a weekend, a subscription is fine. If you're going to use it for years — which most people who adopt AI end up doing — the one-time model is dramatically cheaper.

## Cost isn't the only thing you're buying

The money is the obvious difference, but it's not the only one. When you [run AI locally](/blog/run-ai-privately-no-coding), your data never leaves your computer — there's no cloud server storing your chats, no account tying your work to your name, and nothing that [could be used to train a future model](/blog/is-it-safe-to-put-client-data-in-ai). For anyone handling confidential or [privileged material](/blog/chatgpt-confidential-documents), that privacy is often worth more than the subscription savings.

So the real comparison isn't just "$20 a month vs $49.99 once." It's:

- **A recurring bill** vs. **a one-time purchase**
- **Your data on someone else's servers** vs. **your data staying on your machine**
- **Access that stops when you stop paying** vs. **software you own for good**

## The bottom line

If you only need AI occasionally and privacy isn't a concern, a monthly subscription is a reasonable place to start. But if you use AI regularly, care about keeping your work private, or simply dislike the idea of paying rent on a tool forever, the math and the privacy both point the same direction: **pay once, run it yourself, and keep both your money and your data.**
