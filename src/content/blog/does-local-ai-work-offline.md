Here's a question that gets to the heart of what [local AI](/blog/local-ai-vs-cloud-ai) actually is: *does it still work when you're offline?* With cloud tools like ChatGPT, the answer is simple and unforgiving — no internet, no AI. Close the laptop lid on a flight, drive somewhere with no signal, or lose your Wi-Fi for an afternoon, and the AI you were relying on is just gone. Local AI flips that completely. Once it's on your computer, it works whether you're connected or not. Let's unpack why — and why it matters more than it first sounds.

## Why cloud AI needs the internet (and local AI doesn't)

When you type a message into a cloud tool, your words travel over the internet to a company's servers, the AI thinks there, and the answer travels back. The "brain" lives somewhere else. No connection means no way to reach it — the same way a video call fails the moment your Wi-Fi drops.

Local AI works the opposite way. When you [run AI on your own computer](/blog/run-ai-privately-no-coding), the [model](/blog/which-local-ai-model-should-you-run) — the actual trained brain — is downloaded and lives *on your machine*. Your question never leaves the building. The computer in front of you does the thinking, reads your message, and writes the reply, all locally. The internet was only ever needed once: to download the model in the first place. After that, you can pull the plug and it keeps right on working.

That's the whole reason the [privacy story](/blog/is-it-safe-to-put-client-data-in-ai) holds together, too. Something that genuinely runs with no connection *can't* be quietly sending your conversations anywhere — there's nowhere for them to go.

## What "works offline" actually looks like

It's worth being concrete, because "offline AI" can sound like a stripped-down, limited version. It isn't. When your connection is gone, a local AI can still:

- **Draft and edit writing** — emails, reports, posts, letters — at full quality.
- **Summarize and rewrite** text you paste in, or [answer questions about your own documents](/blog/chat-with-your-documents-privately).
- **Brainstorm, explain, and reason** through a problem, the same as it does online.
- **Hold a normal back-and-forth conversation**, remembering what you said earlier in the chat.

The one honest limit: a local AI doesn't have live access to the internet, so it can't look up today's news, check a live webpage, or fetch the current weather. What it *knows* comes from its training, plus whatever you give it. For the vast majority of everyday work — writing, thinking, organizing, understanding — that's exactly what you need, and none of it requires a signal.

## Where this quietly changes things

Once you have an AI that doesn't depend on a connection, a few situations stop being a problem:

- **On a plane.** A long flight is prime focused-work time, and it's exactly when cloud AI abandons you. A local model doesn't notice you're at 36,000 feet.
- **Traveling or working remotely.** Cafés with flaky Wi-Fi, trains through tunnels, a cabin with no bars, another country with spotty data — none of it interrupts you.
- **Spotty or slow connections.** Even when you technically *have* internet, a weak connection makes cloud tools stutter and time out. Local AI answers at a steady pace regardless, because nothing is being sent anywhere.
- **Sensitive environments.** Some workplaces — clinics, law offices, secure facilities — restrict what can be sent over the internet for good reason. An AI that runs [entirely on the machine](/blog/is-it-safe-to-put-client-data-in-ai) sidesteps that entirely.

There's also a subtler benefit: **it's always there.** No outages, no "service is temporarily unavailable," no rate limits when a provider is busy. Your AI is a program on your computer, as dependable as your word processor.

## The one time you'll want a connection

To be straight with you: you do need the internet *once*, up front. When you first set things up, the software downloads your AI model — that's a one-time download of a file, a bit like installing any other app. After that, it's yours to run offline as much as you like. If you later want to try a bigger or newer model, you'll go online again just long enough to fetch it. Day to day, though, you're free.

## The bottom line

Cloud AI is only ever as reliable as your internet connection. Local AI cuts that cord: download it once, and it works on a plane, in a dead zone, or during an outage, at full quality — because the thinking happens on your own machine and your words never have to travel anywhere.
